  • The Aqaba Ridge site lies adjacent to the Jericho‑Jerusalem highway, a historic supply line for both civilian and military traffic.

International Legal Framework

  • UN Security Council Resolution 242 (1967) calls for withdrawal from territories occupied in the Six‑Day War.

Furthermore, any legal challenge must reference these instruments to maintain legitimacy under international law.

Resource and Environmental Impact

  • Satellite‑derived hydrological models indicate the Aqaba Ridge project will occupy 12 km² of the Jordan Valley catchment.
  • Projected water diversion could reduce downstream flow to Palestinian farms by 15 % during the dry season.
  • The Ministry’s environmental impact statement (EIS) currently underestimates evapotranspiration losses by 40 %.

Agricultural Implications

  • The Jordan Valley supplies irrigation for ≈ 45 % of Palestinian agricultural output.
  • A 15 % reduction translates to a loss of ≈ 3,200 tonnes of produce annually, affecting food security and export revenues.
  • NGOs estimate a US$ 45 million economic shortfall for Palestinian farmers if mitigation measures are not implemented.

Climate Resilience Considerations

  • Climate projections for 2030 predict a 5 % decrease in regional precipitation.
  • Infrastructure that reduces water availability will exacerbate drought vulnerability.
  • Integrating rainwater harvesting and grey‑water recycling into the settlement design could offset up to 30 % of the projected deficit.

Therefore, a comprehensive resource‑impact assessment is essential before contract award.

Background & Historical Context

To understand this bid, one must trace the territorial shifts in the West Bank since 1967. The region is a legal mosaic of war, accords, and unilateral decrees.

Historical Timeline & Precedents

  • 1967 – Six‑Day War – Israel captured the West Bank, Gaza, the Golan Heights, and Sinai, establishing military rule.
  • 1978 – Camp David Accords – These introduced “land for peace,” a principle that still haunts West Bank negotiations.
  • 1993 – Oslo I Accord – This created the Palestinian Authority (PA) and split the West Bank into Areas A, B, and C. Area C (≈60% of the land) remains under total Israeli control.
  • 2005 – Disengagement Plan – Israel left Gaza but doubled down on West Bank settlements, signaling a move toward de-facto annexation.
  • 2020 – Abraham Accords – Normalization with the UAE and Bahrain gave Israel new diplomatic cover, turning settlements into a bargaining chip.
  • 2023 – Annexation Proposals – The coalition government floated a limited annexation of the Jordan Valley. This bid serves as a preemptive "development" strike.
