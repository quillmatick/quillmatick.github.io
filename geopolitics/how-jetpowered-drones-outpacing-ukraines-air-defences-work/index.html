Impact on Ukrainian Air Defences

Declining interception success

Earlier this year Ukraine showed relative success in countering Russian drones and was intercepting 94% of long-range drones, according to Zelensky.

But the speed of the newer drones has prompted the latest round in Kyiv and Moscow's arms race.

Ukraine's Air Force data showed a drop in interceptions of ballistic missiles up to the end of July.

In August, Kyiv mostly stopped publishing the figures that would show its interception rate.

These trends suggest that the surge in high-speed drones is already depressing overall defensive performance.

Ukrainian response: new missile development

On 10 August, Ukraine's deputy head of military intelligence, Maj Gen Vadym Skibitskyi, said his forces needed new interceptor missiles "capable of matching [jet-drones'] speed".

Four Ukrainian companies have reportedly started testing missiles that can shoot down the new Geran models, officials in Kyiv recently told reporters.

The country's new defence minister, Yevhenii Khmara, has tasked them with producing "thousands of units" as quickly as possible.

Consequently, Kyiv is accelerating procurement and domestic production to field missiles that can engage targets moving at jet-drone velocities.

Strategic and Economic Implications

Blurred distinction between drones and cruise missiles

Russia also had an economic incentive to invest in the jet-powered drones.

The increased speed and manoeuvrability of these models means the line between them and more expensive cruise missiles has become "blurred", said John Hardie of the Foundation for Defense of Democracies, a think tank focused on national security and foreign policy.

"Long-range one-way attack drones are basically a poor man's cruise missile," he said.

"The more you add capability to a drone, the less meaningful the distinction between the drone and a lower-end cruise missile."

Thus, Moscow can field a large quantity of relatively cheap systems that achieve many of the same effects as pricier missiles, stretching the cost-effectiveness calculus in its favour.

CAR’s assessment of capabilities and vulnerabilities

But the analyst organisation Conflict Armament Research emphasised that jet-drones are not yet as advanced as cruise missiles.

It said the munitions have a shorter attack range, carry much smaller warheads and are more susceptible to jamming systems.

Therefore, while the drones complicate defence, they remain vulnerable to electronic warfare measures that can disrupt their guidance and communications.

Infrastructure and Launch Sites

Tsymbulova base development and location

Meanwhile, satellite images appear to show Moscow is redesigning key facilities dedicated to fighting the war in Ukraine to host jet-powered drones, with longer rails associated with the munitions visible at an entirely new base at Tsymbulova in the Oryol region.

Multiple launch sites appear to have been built at the facility, which is around 109 miles (175 km) from the Ukrainian border.

Since the facility became operational, Tsymbulova appears to have become a key centre for Russian drone attacks.

Oryol region, where the Tsymbulova base is located, was not mentioned as a drone launch site in Air Force reports before September 2024, but by August 2026 it was named in almost every attack.

The proximity of the rail-based launch pads to the front line shortens flight times, further enhancing the drones' ability to outrun defensive reaction cycles.

Broader Operational Context

Integrated drone-missile assaults

The proliferation of jet-powered drones comes as BBC Verify found that Russia is now striking Ukraine with complex attacks involving hundreds of drones and missiles about once a week.

The increase in the size of the strikes is being powered by Moscow's shift towards combining a "greater variety of munitions" - including jet-powered and decoy drones - with expensive ballistic missiles that are extremely difficult to intercept, analyst Michael Bohnert of the RAND think tank told BBC Verify.

Kyiv's data shows that Moscow is now using over 15 times as many drones per attack than it was four years ago.

Bohnert added that the tactically complicated nature of the assaults, coupled with a global shortage of air defence systems capable of halting ballistic missiles partly caused by the US-Israeli war with Iran, has led to falling interception rates by Ukraine.

Consequently, the Russian campaign blends high-speed drones with conventional ballistic weapons, creating multi-layered threats that strain detection, tracking, and engagement resources.

Notable incidents and regional spill-

According to Zelensky, the drones were used as part of a wave of attacks that killed at least 12 people and injured 20 more on 1 September.

Six of the casualties were railway workers after a depot was hit in Kyiv.

And on Wednesday the Moldovan government said a plane carrying Zelensky was delayed at Chisinau airport on Tuesday after a Russian drone - reported to be jet-powered - entered Moldovan airspace.

These events underscore that the drone campaign not only inflicts civilian harm but also risks diplomatic friction with neighbouring states when airspace is violated.
